## Start with the official syllabus

Print or save the official AKTU syllabus for your scheme and semester. Mark the units,
credits, and marks distribution. Do not rely on a coaching handout for subject codes or
unit coverage; use the official document as the final authority.

## Build a one-page unit map

For each unit, write the topic list in your own words and add three columns: what I know,
what I can practise, and what I need to ask. This turns a long syllabus into a short
working list.

## Study in 2-5-10 blocks

Prepare short definitions for 2-mark questions, worked explanations for 5-mark questions,
and structured answers for 10-mark questions. Keep one example and one diagram per major
topic. Diagrams should be your own, not traced from a book.

## Use previous papers as a map, not a shortcut

Read the question stems, tag each question by unit and topic, and note repeats. Write your
own solutions. Do not memorise a scanned answer key; the goal is to recognise the pattern
and build an answer you can defend.

## Revise on a schedule

- Day 1: read the unit map and write definitions.
- Day 3: solve three 5-mark questions without notes.
- Day 7: attempt a timed 10-mark answer.
- Day 14: review mistakes and rewrite the weakest answer.

## Exam-day checklist

Carry your admit card, ID, pens, and a transparent geometry box if allowed. Read the whole
paper first, start with the questions you can answer confidently, and leave time to check
units, formulas, and question numbers. Follow the official AKTU instructions on the day.
